About

I am a tenure-track assistant professor in the Department of Electrical and Computer Engineering at University of Miami, starting Fall 2026. I am also a research associate at Harvard. Previously, I was a Postdoctoral Fellow at Harvard University, working closely with Marinka Zitnik at Harvard and Manolis Kellis at MIT. I received my PhD degree from Northeastern University, supervised by Prof. Yanzhi Wang. Prior to that, I earned my master degree from Northeastern University and B.E. degree with honors from Huazhong University of Science and Technology

I was a research intern at Microsoft Research, ARM, and Samsung Research. I am a recipient of Machine Learning and Systems Rising Stars 2024.

Research

My research aims to make advanced AI both more efficient and autonomous, enabling scalable intelligence and accelerating scientific discovery.

  • Efficient AI
    • Foundation Models & Agents: Developing efficient LLM，VLM and agentic architectures for planning, memory, long-context modeling, adaptive reasoning, and efficient training and inference.
    • Model & Deployment Efficiency: Improving practical AI deployment through model compression, quantization, sparsity, KV-cache optimization, memory-efficient architectures, and hardware–software co-design under resource constraints.
  • AI for Science
    • Multimodal Foundation & World Models: Developing multimodal representations and models for complex scientific systems, including scientific data integration, state representation, prediction, generation, and simulation.
    • AI Scientists & Scientific Agents: Building agents for scientific reasoning, hypothesis generation, planning, experimental design, and closed-loop discovery, with applications in single-cell and spatial genomics, drug discovery, and related domains.

Token Reduction Should Go Beyond Efficiency in Generative Models -- From Vision, Language to Multimodality
Zhenglun Kong*, Yize Li*, Fanhu Zeng, Lei Xin, Shvat Messica, Xue Lin, Pu Zhao, Manolis Kellis, Hao Tang, Marinka Zitnik

We argue that viewing token reduction purely from an efficiency perspective is fundamentally limited. Instead, we position token reduction as a core design principle in generative modeling.

Enabling Flexible Multi-LLM Integration for Scalable Knowledge Aggregation

TxAgent: an AI agent for therapeutic reasoning across a universe of tools

SPATIA: Multimodal Generation and Prediction of Spatial Cell Phenotypes
Zhenglun Kong*, Mufan Qiu*, John Boesen, Xiang Lin, Sukwon Yun, Tianlong Chen, Manolis Kellis, Marinka Zitnik
[ICML 2026] International Conference on Machine Learning
PDF / Code

We introduce SPATIA, a multi-scale generative and predictive model for spatial transcriptomics. SPATIA learns cell-level embeddings by fusing image-derived morphological tokens and transcriptomic vector tokens and then aggregates them at niche and tissue levels to capture spatial dependencies.

Exploring Token Pruning in Vision State Space Models
Zheng Zhan*, Zhenglun Kong*, Yifan Gong, Yushu Wu, Zichong Meng, Hangyu Zheng, Xuan Shen, Stratis Ioannidis, Wei Niu, Pu Zhao, Yanzhi Wang
[NeurIPS 2024] Advances in Neural Information Processing Systems
PDF / Code

We revisit the unique computational characteristics of SSMs and discover that naive application disrupts the sequential token positions. This insight motivates us to design a novel and general token pruning method specifically for SSM-based vision models.

We propose a tailored, unified post-training token reduction method for SSMs. Our approach integrates token importance and similarity, thus taking advantage of both pruning and merging, to devise a fine-grained intra-layer token reduction strategy.

Data Level Lottery Ticket Hypothesis for Vision Transformers

[IJCAI 2023 Oral] The 32nd International Joint Conference on Artificial Intelligence
PDF / Code

We generalize the LTH for ViTs to input data consisting of image patches inspired by the input dependence of ViTs. That is, there exists a subset of input image patches such that a ViT can be trained from scratch by using only this subset of patches and achieve similar accuracy to the ViTs trained by using all image patches.
SPViT: Enabling Faster Vision Transformers via Latency-aware Soft Token Pruning

Efficient Transformer-based Large Scale Language Representations using Hardware-friendly Block Structured Pruning

[EMNLP 2020 Findings] Conference on Empirical Methods in Natural Language Processing
PDF / Code
